## What is an MCP server?

MCP stands for Model Context Protocol. It is an open standard that lets AI assistants connect to outside tools and data in a consistent way, instead of every product inventing its own one-off integration.

An MCP server is the part that exposes a specific system to the assistant. The Yonovo MCP server exposes your accounts receivable data: the invoices, the aging, the payment history, and the customer context that lives inside Yonovo. When you ask Claude a question, it can read from that server to ground its answer in your actual numbers.

The short version: MCP is the secure, standardized bridge between your AI assistant and the systems where your real work lives. Yonovo built the bridge for accounts receivable.

## Why we built it grounded in real AR data

[Accounts receivable](/blog/what-is-ar-automation) is not only an automation problem. It is a relationship problem. A reminder sent at the wrong moment, or a wrong assumption about why a customer is late, can damage trust that took years to build.

That is why the Yonovo MCP server does not hand an AI assistant a vague summary and ask it to improvise. It connects to the same complete record your team already works from. Every answer is anchored to specific invoices, specific dates, and specific history.

Generic AI applied to finance produces confident noise. AI grounded in your actual receivables produces answers you can act on. The difference is the data underneath, and that is exactly what the MCP server plugs into.

## What the Yonovo MCP server unlocks

Once connected, your AI assistant can read your Yonovo data and answer questions instantly. Here is what that looks like across the roles on a finance team.

### For AR managers

- Who should I prioritize this week?
- Which accounts represent the highest risk right now?
- Show me customers with the largest overdue balances.

The assistant returns a ranked list built from outstanding amounts, aging, and recent history, so your priority list is clear without building a single report.

### For controllers

- What is blocking payment on this account?
- Why is this customer consistently late?
- Are there open disputes or missing contacts here?

The assistant surfaces disputes, broken promises to pay, and gaps in the communication timeline, then summarizes the situation so you can tell a customer-side problem from an internal process gap.

### For CFOs and finance leaders

- Give me a cash flow view for this week.
- How much can we realistically expect to collect next week?
- Where is our receivables risk concentrated?

Instead of reading off due dates, the assistant reasons over [actual payment behavior](/blog/can-ai-predict-which-customers-will-pay-late) to give you a more honest picture of what is likely to land, and when.

When your assistant answers a question, it is drawing from a complete financial record and real interaction history, not a thin slice of exported rows. ## Read-only by design

The Yonovo MCP server is read-only. It lets your AI assistant read your accounts receivable data to answer questions, and nothing more. It cannot change an invoice, send a message, or take any action on your account.

That boundary is deliberate. Asking a question should never carry the risk of altering your ledger. You get the speed of a conversation with the safety of knowing the assistant is reading, never writing.
