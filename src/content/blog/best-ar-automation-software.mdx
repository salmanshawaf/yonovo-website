## What to evaluate in AR automation software

Selecting AR automation software requires more than a feature checklist. Focus on the outcomes that strengthen cash flow, reduce manual effort, and scale with your operations. These are the criteria that separate tools that work from tools that look good in a demo.

### Integration depth

The platform must connect to your accounting system with real-time, two-way sync. It should pull invoice and customer data automatically and write payment status back without manual intervention. One-way sync or daily batch imports create data mismatches that erode trust in the system.

### Automation scope

Basic tools send email reminders on a schedule. Better tools let you build multi-step workflows with escalation logic: friendly reminder at day 1, firmer tone at day 14, phone call at day 30, and escalation to management at day 45. The best tools handle payment matching automatically when money arrives.

### Multi-channel outreach

Email alone has diminishing returns after the second or third reminder. Look for platforms that support SMS, payment portal links, or automated calling in addition to email. Different customers respond to different channels.

### Operating model and headcount impact

Decide who will run credit review, reminders, and collections follow-ups. Some platforms are software you configure and staff yourself. Others, like Yonovo, are designed to run collections hands-off so you do not have to add headcount as you scale.

### Time to value and total cost of ownership

A tool that takes three months to implement delays your ROI by three months. For SMBs and mid-market teams, implementation should take days, not months. Look past the sticker price to the full cost: implementation fees, the internal hours to run it, and how long until it pays for itself.

### Reporting and visibility

You need to track [DSO trends](/blog/how-to-reduce-dso), collection effectiveness, and aging buckets over time. The platform should make it obvious whether your AR is getting healthier or worse, without requiring someone to build custom reports.

## Implementation and pricing at a glance

**Implementation** is the factor most teams underestimate. SMB and mid-market tools like Yonovo, Chaser, and FreshBooks go live in days. Mid-market platforms like Quadient AR, Upflow, Gaviti, and Invoiced take a few weeks. Enterprise suites like HighRadius, Esker, and Sidetrade take months and require IT and finance to work together. If you do not have a project team to spare, that timeline is a real cost, not a footnote.

**Pricing** falls into three bands. Entry SMB tools start around $20 to $80 per month. Mid-market platforms typically land in the $200 to $500 per month range or custom pricing. Enterprise platforms are custom and can reach five figures per month. The number that matters is not the monthly fee but the payback: how many days of DSO the tool removes, multiplied by your monthly revenue.

**Start with your accounting system.** If you use QuickBooks Online, Xero, Odoo, NetSuite, Sage, FreshBooks, Salesforce, HubSpot, or SAP, [Yonovo](/solutions/quickbooks) integrates directly. **Test the sync before committing.** Ask for a trial and connect it to your real data. Run it for a week and verify that invoice status, payment matching, and customer records stay in sync. This single test will tell you more than any demo.

**Calculate the ROI before you buy.** Take your current DSO, multiply your monthly revenue by each day of DSO reduction, and compare that to the software cost.
