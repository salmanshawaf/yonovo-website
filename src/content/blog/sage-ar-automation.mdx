Sage Intacct is a favorite among finance teams that need real dimensional reporting and clean multi-entity accounting. It is strong at recording what happened. What it does not do is actively collect what you are owed. Sage handles invoicing, customer records, and statements, but the work of chasing overdue invoices across channels, prioritizing at-risk accounts, and handling disputes is still manual.

This guide covers what to automate in Sage accounts receivable and how to do it without a drawn-out rollout. If you are still weighing platforms, the [accounts receivable automation software guide](/accounts-receivable-automation-software) compares ten of them side by side.

## What Sage handles natively

On the AR side, Sage Intacct covers the system-of-record essentials:

- Invoicing and customer records
- Dimensional AR reporting and aging
- Scheduled statements and basic reminders
- Strong multi-entity and consolidation support

These are the foundations. But Sage will not run a multi-touch follow-up sequence that escalates as an invoice ages, predict which customers are likely to pay late, or pause when a customer disputes a charge. That active collections layer is what [AR automation](/blog/what-is-ar-automation) adds.

## What to automate

1. **Follow-up cadences.** Move from ad hoc reminders to a defined sequence that fires before and after the due date.
2. **Multi-channel outreach.** Email alone loses steam as invoices age; SMS, voice, and WhatsApp reach more customers. See [multi-channel payment chasing](/blog/multi-channel-payment-chasing).
3. **Risk-based prioritization.** Focus your team on the accounts most likely to slip.
4. **Dispute handling.** Flag disputes, pause automation, and route to a human with context.
5. **Status write-back.** Sync payment status to Sage so you are not reconciling two systems.

## Multi-entity collections

Organizations choose Sage Intacct largely for multi-entity accounting, so your collections layer should match. Confirm that invoices and aging sync per entity and that workflows can be segmented by entity, currency, or customer, rather than collapsing everything into a single queue.
